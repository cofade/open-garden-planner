# Sun and shade

## Solar engine, shadows and sun maps

- **FR-SUN-01**: The application computes the sun's position (geometric + refraction-corrected elevation, compass azimuth clockwise from true north, declination, equation of time, hour angle) for any site latitude/longitude (+east) and any timezone-aware UTC instant, to within ±0.5° (NOAA/Meeus formulation, actual accuracy ±0.1° class for 1900–2100). Naive datetimes are rejected with a clear error. Exclusions (stated, Phase 14): downstream shadow features use the *geometric* elevation (refraction ≤0.02° above 30° elevation is irrelevant at garden scale); site `elevation_m` does not shift on-site shadows; terrain slopes are out of scope.
- **FR-SUN-02**: Every height-bearing object resolves an **effective above-ground height** with the precedence: explicit user-set height → container fill height (`container_height_cm`, never aliased — it keeps meaning soil fill) → assigned species' `max_height_cm` → a per-type default (fence 120 cm, wall 200, hedge 150, house 450, garage/tool shed 250, greenhouse 220, trellis 180, raised bed 40, tables 75, chairs/benches 85, lounger 80, BBQ 90, compost bin 100; every object kind has one since L4's owner passes — the full table is `DEFAULT_HEIGHTS_CM`, see FR-3D-BUILT-01) → none (object casts no shadow until it is given a height: the ground surfaces, generic shapes, annotations and a plant without species data). The properties panel shows an **"Object height:"** field for EVERY item with a footprint (the defaults-table types, containers, plants, generic shapes, and the ground-surface types - lawn, path, terrace, driveway, pond, in-ground bed - because the field is the only way to set one and may not be gated on one already existing, #628, and including species-less placeholders): it displays the resolved effective height with a tooltip naming its source ("Custom height" / "From container fill height" / "From assigned species" / "Default for this object type"); typing a value stores an explicit override, setting **0 ("Auto")** clears it back to automatic. Each edit is **one undoable step** and marks the plan dirty. A height also makes the item a 3D solid, which shows the item's own fill on its faces — its fill pattern tinted with its fill colour, or that colour alone for SOLID (FR-3D-BUILT-09).
- **FR-SUN-03**: With the permanent solar simulation (separate from decorative drop shadows), the canvas shows a semi-transparent overlay of the unioned ground shadows of every visible object with an effective height (FR-SUN-02), computed analytically (`L = h / tan α`, footprint swept opposite the sun's azimuth) for the simulated instant and the project's garden location. Shadows recompute automatically (debounced) when items move/resize/rotate, heights change, the sim time changes, or the location changes — never during painting. The overlay renders above the background image and the **ground surfaces** — a lawn, path, terrace, driveway, pond or in-ground bed without a height of its own, which the shadow lies ON — and below everything else (FR-STACK-09) (manual pass of PR #584: a lawn used to hide it); it appears in PNG/PDF exports while enabled, and is never written to the `.ogp`; it is clipped to the canvas bounds (cut off at the garden edge, never spilling onto the grey area outside) and drawn with a soft edge: a smooth falloff from the shade's own tone to nothing 7 cm outside it, with no band at any zoom or in a 300-dpi picture, and **nowhere darker than the shade itself** — not beside a straight edge and not in an inside corner, in the cusp between two shades, in a sunlit hole or in a narrow sunlit gap, where sunlit ground is painted at most in the shade's own tone (where shades of different strength meet, at most in the darkest one's within 7 cm); the soft edges on the two sides of a sunlit gap meet in it, so a gap of up to 4 cm between two shades is painted in the shade's tone, a wider one lightens towards its middle (0.82 of the tone at 6 cm, 0.42 at 10 cm) and from 14 cm on its middle is clear. An enclosed sunlit hole 8 cm across can also be painted at full shade tone. The computed shadows, 3D drape and hours-of-sun map use the underlying geometry (#880) (#793; until then four stacked strokes, which showed as stripes from 200 % zoom on, and after them a first falloff that was up to 41 of 97 levels darker than the shade in such places). While the hours-of-sun map is shown (FR-SUN-05) the overlay is **not painted**, on the canvas and in exports: the shadows are still computed, and the 3D view's shadow drape follows the time of day as before (#794). Empty states: no garden location → overlay off with a hint naming File → Set Garden Location; sun below 0.5° geometric elevation → the whole canvas is shaded (night) with a "night" hint. Exclusions (stated, Phase 14): flat ground (site elevation and terrain slopes ignored), geometric (unrefracted) sun elevation, opaque objects only (no canopy translucency). A plant that stands on raised soil casts from that soil, not from the ground: **FR-SUN-11**.
- **FR-SUN-04**: The permanent combined Plan & Sun toolbar provides one calendar, monthly scrubber, a Today button that says Past or Future in its own place while the plan date is elsewhere (one width for all three texts, so nothing after it moves; a click sets the current date and time in the plan's zone, even when the plan already shows today's date), editable time, a time-of-day slider (1-minute steps, the plan's zone when it names one, else the computer's), and an **Animate** toggle that advances the time slider automatically (10 sim-minutes per 200 ms tick, wrapping at midnight). Solar simulation is always active and defaults to the current date and time on each app start — the sim instant is not persisted across sessions, so a fresh run always reflects today. **One simulation clock** (Phase 17 L1.0, ADR-053) holds the chosen moment for the whole app. The shadow overlay (FR-SUN-03), the hours-of-sun heatmap (FR-SUN-05) and the 3D view (FR-SUN-06) all read the same **plan date** — the local calendar day the toolbar shows, which also drives growth (FR-SUN-08) — and the same instant for the sun position. A time-of-day change (dragging the slider, animating, or wrapping past midnight, which stays on the same date) never counts as a date change: it moves the sun, keeps a computed heatmap, and does not rebuild the 3D geometry. The date picker accepts 1971-01-01 to 2999-12-31, the clock's supported range on every platform (bounded by the system timezone conversions that first set it). When the plan's location names an IANA time zone (#945, ADR-053 addendum), the toolbar's wall time, the sun, a camera tour's time range and the Past/Today/Future state read that zone instead of the computer's; a plan without one keeps the computer's zone, and changing the zone keeps the wall reading (the date and time of day) while the instant moves. Around a DST changeover the slider's wall clock always means the first occurrence: in the skipped spring hour it shows the sun of the hour after it, and the repeated autumn hour shows its first occurrence (a session started during the second one steps the sun back about an hour on the next slider move); the date never changes.
- **FR-SUN-05**: An **"Hours of Sun"** button on the simulation toolbar enables automatic background updates of a full-day direct-sun heatmap for the shown date: the daylight period is sampled every 15 minutes (UTC civil day), each sample's shadow union (FR-SUN-03 machinery) is rasterized over a 10 cm grid covering the canvas, and per-cell sun minutes are rendered as a **smooth continuous cool→warm surface** (darker = fewer sun hours, fading to nearly transparent in full sun) overlaid with **topographic contour lines every hour, labeled on even hours (2 h / 4 h / 6 h…)**. The map carries a **key** (#794; until then the labels were its only key): a colour bar of the map's own tints from no sun to sun all day, with a mark at every hour the map contours, the even hours numbered with their unit in the application's language (every second or third of them, evenly, where room is short) and a title naming what the bar measures and the day the map is of, the day written as every date in the application's texts is (day, month name, year, in the application's language: "21 Jun 2026", "21. Juni 2026"). On the canvas the key lies over the bottom-left corner of the view, in the strip of the scale bar, at every zoom and pan; it keeps clear of the scale bar and the minimap (without its title, then one strip higher, where the view is narrow) and reads in the light and the dark theme on any ground. Every picture of the plan with the map on — PNG, SVG, PDF report, print, the Agent API's `render_canvas_image` — carries the key in the plan's south-west corner, sized with the picture's text. The key is there exactly while a map is shown, including a map kept on screen as a dated preview, and is never written to the `.ogp`. The **hour labels** are drawn above plants and objects, stand where their contour is in the open (a place a plant or an object stands on, or at the plan's edge, is passed over for the nearest free one along the line; a contour that is covered all the way has no label) and are left out where they would touch another label or an object's name. The horticultural bands (deep shade <2 h, light shade 2–4 h, partial sun 4–6 h, full sun ≥6 h) remain the domain classification the even-hour contours align with. The computation runs in a worker thread — the UI stays responsive throughout — with a dated Updating indication and an operable Off button. The overlay renders on top of the ground surfaces and under everything else (FR-SUN-03), and while it is shown the time-of-day shadow overlay is not painted: the map is the day's total and translucent, and the shadow of one instant under it would read as fewer hours of sun (#794). It appears in exports while shown, and is never written to the `.ogp`. While checked, date and exposure-input edits refresh automatically; an old map stays labelled with its actual date and Updating until replaced. Superseded results never become current measurements. Time-only animation preserves the full-day map. Unchecking clears it; document/location/grid changes discard old imagery. Visual exports wait for current exposure with cancellation and a 30-second deadline (ADR-062). Empty states and exclusions follow FR-SUN-03 (no location → hint; flat ground; geometric elevation clamped to ≥0.5° for grazing dawn/dusk samples). Season analysis = run the map for representative dates (equinoxes/solstices) via the date picker; no multi-date averaging in Phase 14 (stated exclusion). Plants on raised soil cast from their soil here exactly as in the overlay (**FR-SUN-11**): both use the same casters. A caster's footprint set is ONE region: the stroke of a fence, wall or hedge whose centre line closes on itself casts its stroke and not the slab it encloses, and an outline that crosses itself is shaded as the lobes it draws (issue #444, ADR-037 addendum "one caster's ring set is one REGION"). The self-crossing half is bounded at 256 vertices: above it such an outline is swept as one ring, exactly as before (a crafted outline pays no simplification on every walk).
- **FR-SUN-06**: **View → "3D View"** shows the plan in 3D inside the main window: from Plan mode it switches the workspace to **Split** — the plan and the 3D view side by side — and in 3D or Split mode it changes nothing (the workspace's own modes Plan / 3D / Split, Alt+1 / Alt+2 / Alt+3, are FR-3D-WS-01; no separate window opens). Every visible object with a footprint stands at its **effective height** (FR-SUN-02) on the same footprint the 2D shadow overlay uses — a building with its roof, a fence with its posts and pickets, a wall, a bed, a hedge as built shapes, a plant as the silhouette of its sprite's archetype (FR-3D-PLANT-01…04), anything else as a prism in its fill colour; objects without a height (lawns, paths, in-ground beds …) lie on the ground as on the plan (the ground bake, FR-3D-GROUND-*) — on a ground the size of the canvas, in a meadow. The camera orbits (FR-3D-WS-06) and starts framed on the plan. The light is the plan's sun — location, date and time of the one simulation clock (FR-SUN-04) — with shadows whose ground coverage matches the 2D overlay's (FR-3D-LOOK-01, FR-3D-LOOK-06); without a garden location a fixed southern midday sun (50° high, azimuth 180°) lights it, and after sunset the view fades through twilight into a moonlit night. The view follows **every** edit of the plan at once (FR-3D-WS-03) — there is no Refresh — and a date change regrows the dated plants (FR-SUN-08). It is a viewer: a click selects (FR-3D-SELECT-01…05), nothing is edited in 3D, and nothing 3D is written to the `.ogp` except the camera tours the user chose to keep in the plan (FR-3D-TOUR-04; camera, mode and walk state are not saved with the plan; the Split position is a window setting). Detail: FR-33.
- **FR-SUN-07**: **"Walk"** in the 3D pane puts the user into the garden at **eye height** — 165 cm by default; the arrow beside the button offers 150 cm and 110 cm — where the view was looking. **W A S D / the arrow keys** walk (Shift runs), **dragging the mouse** looks around, **Escape** (or the Walk button) returns to orbit with the previous orbit view restored exactly. The walker cannot leave the plan and cannot leave its eye height (no flying, no crouching), and it has a body: walls, fences, buildings, hedges, raised beds and containers stop it (FR-3D-WALK-02; plants and furniture do not). The sun and the simulation's date and time stay live while walking. Flat ground only (terrain is a Phase 14 exclusion); nothing of walking is written to the `.ogp`. Detail: FR-3D-WALK-01…03.
- **FR-SUN-08**: A plant's **measured current size** — the existing Plant Details **Current height** / **Current spread** fields — is what the sun/shade features use: its shadow (US-E3), hours-of-sun contribution (US-E4) and 3D model (US-E6, FR-3D-PLANT-02) all follow the measured height and canopy instead of the species' mature maximum. Both fields are read as literal centimetres — a 150 cm spread casts a 150 cm-wide canopy shadow, and (since issue #299) the SAME measured size drives the SVG icon's rendered size on the 2D canvas too, so a young plant now visibly looks smaller than its assigned species' mature footprint there as well. The drawn circle's own *geometry* (`rect()`/`radius` — the spacing footprint used for overlap/companion-distance diagnostics) is unaffected and stays on the mature `max_spread_cm` regardless: shrinking it to a sapling's current canopy would let the user plant too densely, so "how much room to reserve" and "how big it currently looks" are two independent things on the same item. A plant measured *beyond* its species maximum keeps its measurement rather than being clamped down to the book value. Measuring **one** dimension implies the other proportionally (a plant measured at a quarter of its mature height is treated as a quarter as wide), so a half-measured plant never renders as a pole or a pancake. The 2D shadow overlay and the 3D view (since Phase 17 L1.9, FR-3D-WS-03) follow the fields live as they are edited; while Hours of Sun is enabled, edits request a debounced refresh and the previous map remains labelled with its actual date until the new result arrives (ADR-062). Given a **planting date** as well (defaulted to the active plan date for every newly placed plant, and editable afterwards), the plant additionally **grows over time**: scrubbing the sim date forward interpolates it linearly from its current size at the planting date up to the species maximum at maturity (years-to-maturity = species `days_to_maturity` when present, else tree 10 y / others 3 y / annuals ≈ a season), consistently across the 2D sprite, shadows, heatmap and 3D from one model and one plan date (the toolbar's local day, FR-SUN-04 — before Phase 17 L1.0 the shadows and 3D used the instant's UTC date, a day off the heatmap's near local midnight). An explicit object-height override always wins. A plant the user has **not** measured keeps the mature size at every date (growth simply stays disengaged) — nothing changes for existing plans. Scrubbing the sun timeline NEVER mutates stored geometry (the drawn footprint's `rect()`/`radius` stay the mature canopy; save files are byte-identical) and the 2D sprite renders its date-projected size from that same plan date. Editing Current height/spread also changes its rendered appearance live. The underlying item geometry remains untouched, so selection/snap/`mapToScene` behave as before. While the date keeps changing — an arrow key held in the date field — the 3D view regrows its dated plants at most twice a second (the first and the last date at once) while the light follows every step (Phase 17 L1.9, #531). **Stated exclusions**: linear growth only (no sigmoid/dormancy/pruning), no separate growth toggle (the sim timeline is the growth timeline).

- **FR-SUN-09**: Cached sun-requirement diagnostics (#314)

  - Explicit Hours of Sun computes the ground heatmap and per-plant soil-plane estimates at sampled footprint centres, excluding the receiving plant's own caster and - for a plant that stands in a soil parent with a height (a raised bed, a container, a mounded bed: linked to it and its centre inside it, FR-SUN-11's membership) - that parent: the bed is under the plant, not between it and the sun. (In v1.30.1 only the plant's own caster was excluded, and every plant in a raised bed read 0 hours and warned.) Since #481 the containment alone spares it: a soil solid (a bed, raised bed, container or wall planter with a height) whose outline contains the receiver's own point is left out for that receiver whether or not the link was made - a bed drawn around an existing plant, a mirror copy without its bed, or a plant moved in with the arrow keys receives the same soil-holder exclusion as a linked one (in an otherwise unobstructed plan, measured: 0.0 h before, 16.5 h after; ADR-056 addendum). It is a per-receiver spare, so a plant outside the bed on its shadow side still loses sun to it, and a solid that cannot hold soil (a wall, a trellis, an in-ground bed) is never spared. Other casters shade the plant at its exact soil elevation from `item_base_cm` (#641): discard solids ending at or below that elevation and slice taller solids above it. Equal elevations share a sweep without rounding. Each caster still starts where it stands (FR-SUN-11); the map remains at ground level. A plant inside a bed's outline WITHOUT a link to it still STANDS on the ground inside the bed's solid, so its own shadow is cast from the ground (FR-SUN-11, issue #443). No computation before opting in to Hours of Sun; no leaf-height or translucent-canopy model. A plant inside a cold frame or planter pot excludes that holder from its shade; external obstacles still affect its sun hours: `ui/canvas/footprints.holds_plants` marks `COLD_FRAME` and `PLANTER_POT` as holding plants, so the per-receiver containment spare leaves their solid out of the plant's shade without false mismatch warnings (issue #621, ADR-065 addendum). A framed glasshouse is NOT spared: glass counts as sun but its frame is between the plant and the sun (FR-SUN-16). Plants outside on the shadow side remain shaded.
  - The single calendar in the permanent Plan & Sun strip owns the reference day. Today sets the current date and time in the plan's zone. While Hours of Sun is enabled, date and exposure-input changes automatically refresh the map after debounce; the last completed map retains its actual date label while updating. Time-only changes preserve exposure. All results and the chosen date remain runtime-only (ADR-062).
  - Nominal full-sun/partial-sun/partial-shade/full-shade bands are >=6/4-6/2-4/<2 hours. Warn only beyond one hour of tolerance: <5; <3 or >7; <1 or >5; >3, respectively. Exact tolerance boundaries do not warn. UNKNOWN never warns. Compare unrounded values, display one decimal, severity warning.
  - A canvas badge is drawn at the resolution it is shown at: sharp at every zoom and display density (#794; until then a 24-pixel picture enlarged with its plant). It is a diagnostic decoration and is in no picture of the plan (FR-VIS-09).
  - Canvas badges, the shared Diagnostics panel and cached unauthenticated `get_diagnostics` agree. `sun_mismatch` details carry `hours`, `required`, ISO `reference_date`, `direction`, `measurement_method=ground_cell_centre_excluding_self`, and `receiver_id`. Enclosing objects are addressable navigation targets for grouped receivers.
  - Missing, cancelled, stale, hidden and out-of-grid evaluations never become zero measurements or passed checks. Known unavailable requirements emit `sun_evaluation` with Information severity, reason and `status=not_evaluated`. Clear mismatches/badges within 100 ms after relevant edits; keep ordinary edited heatmaps only as explicitly stale snapshots. Requirement-only changes may reclassify valid results.
  - A single selected TREE shows Under this canopy: other footprint centres inside/on its recorded mature max_spread_cm/2. Membership ignores guild/layer/bed, excludes the tree and sorts by enum layer, displayed name and UUID. Missing traits are Unspecified; missing mature spread is unavailable. Rows show dated cached hours or an unavailable hint. Geometric inclusion does not establish the cause of shade.

- **FR-SUN-10**: Document-owned heatmap lifecycle (#428)

  `CanvasScene.document_replaced` clears overlays, receiver results, diagnostics, canopy state and the checked toolbar button, cancelling pending work before item teardown. Generation/signature checks reject old queued success. Worker ownership survives until `finished`; a second launch is refused until pending signals complete. This applies to New, validated Open/Recent/Welcome/recovery/direct loads and agent New/Open. Prevalidation failure preserves the active plan and valid exposure. No project-format or version change.

- **FR-SUN-11**: A plant stands on a parent's **soil** when both hold: it is **linked** to that parent (the bed membership the Properties panel shows) and its **centre lies inside the parent's shape** — the centre of the plant's circle itself, which does not move when a warning badge or a highlight is drawn over the plant; the parent is a **raised bed, container, round container or wall planter**, or a garden bed that carries a height. The link alone is not enough: since #443 every geometry edit keeps the link in step with the plant's standing point — creation, moves (arrow keys, typed coordinates, align/distribute, constraints, drags), a bed's create/resize/turn/vertex edits, and paste/duplicate/array/mirror — and the same point answers both the link and the lift (ADR-037 addendum "bed membership is reconciled by geometry"). An explicit Link action may still link a plant to a bed it is not in (`link_is_geometric: false`); such a plant stands on the ground until its next move re-decides the link. **Where the soil is**: in a **bed**, 2 cm below the rim (the bed's height, FR-SUN-02) — an assumption of the model, since the plan holds no fill level for a bed; in a **container**, AT its resolved height, because that height is its soil fill height (FR-SUN-02: `container_height_cm` means soil fill and stands in for the above-ground height — the plan holds no separate rim, and none is invented). The plant's own height (FR-SUN-02, FR-SUN-08) is measured from the soil: a 60 cm perennial in a 40 cm raised bed occupies 38–98 cm above the ground, in a 30 cm pot 30–90 cm. The shadow overlay (FR-SUN-03) and the hours-of-sun map (FR-SUN-05) cast it from that height: its shadow starts `base / tan α` from its footprint and its tip lies `base / tan α` further from the sun than before (`α` = solar elevation) — for the raised-bed example 54 cm further at a 35° sun and 142 cm further at 15°. The ground between the plant and the start of its shadow is shaded by the bed, which casts its own shadow as before. **What the user sees change**: before, such a plant cast as if it grew out of the ground *through* its bed, so in the middle of a bed its shadow never left the bed's own and it did not show in the shadow map at all; now the plant's shadow reaches out of the bed's, and the hours-of-sun map counts the shade it casts beyond the bed. Unchanged: every object on the ground, every plant in an in-ground garden bed (no height) or on a trellis (a plant parent that holds no soil), and every plant without a parent. A parent on a hidden layer counts as absent (its plants cast from the ground, like the parent's own shadow disappears). Raising or lowering the bed, resizing it or moving the plant updates the plant's shadow with the next recompute: **where a linked plant stands follows its centre**, whatever moved it — outside its bed's shape it stands on the ground, back inside it stands on the soil again. The LINK is no longer limited: since #443 it is made or removed from geometry by every path above, and by the arrow keys, a typed coordinate, align/distribute, the mirror tool's Move, array copies (which drop the source's link and are linked where they land) and a mirror copy made without its bed. A plant that is in a raised bed without a link can no longer exist in a plan edited by this version — the next edit links it — except through an explicit Link action that geometry does not support, which is the one deliberate non-geometric link. The same rule places the plant in the Living Garden 3D scene data (Phase 17), so the 3D solid and the 2D shadow start at the same height — except where the 3D scene holds no solid for the parent at all (a parent wider than the scene's size limit of 10 km, or one whose outline cannot be built into a solid): there the 3D plant stands on the ground while its 2D shadow still starts from the parent's soil (ADR-037 addendum "base stacking", decision 5). **A garden bed with a height** exists only through its stored metadata (a generated or hand-edited plan, or an item that had a height before its type changed): the Properties panel offers no Height field for a garden bed that has none. **Remaining limits**: parents do not nest - a climber linked to a trellis that stands in a raised bed stays at ground level, because a trellis holds no soil; a pot drawn on a raised bed stands on the ground, since only plants have a parent. Drawing a bed around a plant now LINKS it (#443), so it stands on the bed's soil like a bed-first plan; the one non-geometric state left is an explicit Link action on a plant outside the bed, which stands on the ground until the plant's next move re-decides it.

- **FR-SUN-12**: Pitched-roof and porous-fence analytical shadow model (issue #440)

  - The 2D shadow overlay, hours-of-sun heatmap, per-plant sun estimates, and 3D analysis drape consume a shared geometry-aware caster model matching 3D structural profiles.
  - Ridged buildings (HOUSE, GARAGE_SHED, TOOL_SHED with gable, hip, or shed roofs) project 3D roof and wall triangles, preserving height, pitch, ridge axis, rotation, and overhang. Flat-roof buildings remain on the prism path.
  - Porous fences (PathFenceStyle.WOODEN_FENCE, METAL_FENCE, CHAIN_LINK) compute angle-dependent sunlight transmittance in [0.0, 1.0] based on solar azimuth and elevation relative to the fence segment geometry.
  - Casters are grouped by owner first so connected segments of a single fence do not square attenuation. Overlapping independent casters multiply transmittance (T_total = prod(T_i), occlusion = 1 - T_total). Porous shade overlapping opaque shade remains fully opaque (T = 0, occlusion = 1).
  - Direct sunlight accumulates as sample minutes multiplied by transmittance.
  - Per-sample transmittance arrays (sample_transmittance, float32) are retained alongside binary shade masks for progressive day sweeps, subject to MAX_SAMPLE_SHADE_BYTES = 64 MiB.
  - 3D shadow drape consumes WeightedShadowRegion sequences to interpolate linear RGB vertex multipliers in a single persistent "shade" mesh.
  - All 9 built-world families meet the 10% relative-area ratio (0.90 <= area_ratio <= 1.10) and <= 15 cm reach difference across 15°, 35°, and 60° solar elevations.

- **FR-SUN-13**: Open frames shade as frames (issue #698)

  - A **pergola**, a **trellis**, a **swing**, a **hammock** and a **garden light** are open frames: the 2D shadow overlay, the hours-of-sun heatmap, the per-plant sun estimates and the 3D analysis drape cast their footprint prism weighted by the share of the sun the frame lets through, not as a solid block.
  - The share is computed for the item itself and the sun of the moment — its size, its height, its rotation, the sun's elevation and azimuth — from the same members the 3D view builds (a pergola's from the one layout its 2D picture is drawn from). The prism is trimmed to the frame's own far member tip (#855), so the shade ends where the real shadow ends, not at `height / tan a` past it. It is between 0 and 1; at the default sizes a pergola lets 64–71 % of the sun through, a trellis 59–67 %, a swing 79–82 %, a hammock 57–77 %, a garden light 59–78 % (sun due south at 15°–60°; `scripts/measure_built_world_shadows.py`).
  - Ground under or behind an open frame collects its minutes of sun multiplied by that share; where the shade of two frames overlaps the shares multiply, and a solid's shade stays full shade (FR-SUN-12).
  - The overlay draws an open frame's shade lighter in proportion, with a soft edge of the same strength.
  - A **parasol** is a raised canopy and a **greenhouse** casts its frame alone (FR-SUN-16); low furniture (tables, bench, chair, trampoline, grill, wheelbarrow, bird bath) keeps the solid prism; a string of lights casts no 2D shadow (FR-3D-LIVE-08).
  - For the weighted 2D shadow of each of the five kinds the 3D model's shadow area is within 10 % at 15°, 35° and 60°.

- **FR-SUN-14**: The plan's north orientation (issue #663)

  - **File > Set North Orientation…** stores a plan-level **`north_angle_deg`**: the direction of true north on the drawing, in degrees clockwise from plan-up (0 = up, 90 = right, 135 = down-right). The dialog offers a clickable/draggable compass and a numeric angle; a small `N` arrow on the canvas follows it. The value is additive plan metadata (`FILE_VERSION` unchanged), restored as 0 for old files and kept by a template export; **no object is rotated or moved**.
  - Every solar consumer turns a compass azimuth into the scene frame **once** (`scene_azimuth = azimuth + north_angle`): the 2D shadow overlay, the hours-of-sun sampling, the plant sun-exposure diagnostics, the 3D sun, and the 3D sun path and compass. The PDF report's north arrow applies the same angle as a painter rotation. Setting 0/45/135/270 produces the expected shadow, heatmap, 3D light and report-arrow direction.
  - A change is **one undo step** (`SetPlanNorthCommand`) and invalidates daily results: a heatmap computed for the old north is cleared or refused, never shown as current. The dialog and the command reject a bool, a non-number, a non-finite or a malformed value before any mutation. In a file, a **non-numeric/malformed** value is ignored and the plan opens at 0; a **non-finite number** is refused by the file validator like every other numeric (`plan_io`).

- **FR-SUN-15**: Hours-of-sun map legibility (issue #852)

  - The map's 10 cm cells are bilinearly interpolated inside every cell (`HEATMAP_UPSAMPLE = 2` per axis) into a lossless `Format_ARGB32` image covering the grid's full extent, uploaded to a `QPixmap` (stored premultiplied by Qt) and drawn with smooth scaling (`SmoothTransformation`), so at 400 % the fill reads as a smooth colour field without 10 cm blotches or nearest-neighbor stair-step blocks. The overlay bitmap's own draw adds ~0.5 ms on the family-garden example at fit zoom.
  - The contour lines of a map live in ONE device-cached layer (one blit, rebuilt by Qt on a zoom or export scale change), and the note clip is built only where a note can reach a line; the map's repaint at fit is 1.3–1.5× one with the map hidden (was 3.3–4.0×, measured 10.0 ms against 2.5 ms on family-garden; `tests/perf/test_sun_heatmap_repaint.py`).
  - An hour label keeps a 1.3× clearance around its own box when its place is chosen, so a fixed-pixel label stays off an object's edge when the plan is zoomed out up to 1.3× the nominal 1100 px fit.
  - The sun-mismatch badge paints a theme-contrasting backing (`core.legibility.halo_for` of the active theme's accent — light halo in light theme, dark halo in dark theme) under its warning triangle only, strictly inside its icon bounds, leaving the sun's middle transparent; the icon itself keeps the theme's accent sentinel.
  - A label's place-box, not only its centre distance, decides where an hour label may stand: no two label boxes intersect and no box intersects a standing object's outline (#1008). The centre spacing alone failed on a wide plan, whose label place-box is wider than 160 cm.

- **FR-SUN-16**: A parasol is a raised canopy, a glasshouse is its frame, and a plant does not shade its own cell (issue #830)

  - A **parasol** casts a solid slab at the canopy's own height — its lower edge to its rim (185.6–193.6 cm on the default 150 cm / 250 cm item) — not a prism from the ground. The shade lies away from the pole at a low sun; the thin pole, the cone above the rim and the finial are left out. The 3D model agrees within the truth gate: IoU 0.89 / 0.93 / 0.96 at 15° / 35° / 60°, 3D ÷ 2D area 1.06 / 1.00 / 0.96 (the prism read 0.24 / 0.41 / 0.60). On the balcony example the 21-June map mean is 6.12 h (was 4.57 h; 6.36 h with no parasol).
  - A **glasshouse** casts its aluminium frame alone. The map counts hours of DIRECT sun, so glass counts as sun: a sample behind glass is sunny, while the frame's members shade through the same transmittance path as an open frame (FR-SUN-13), from the same frame triangles the 3D builder casts. 3D ÷ weighted 2D area is 1.00 at all three elevations (was 0.30 / 0.23 / 0.25). A plant inside reads the open sky minus the frame's share, never 0 h; a gabled greenhouse whose builder has no shadow-casting frame is transparent in both models. A framed glasshouse is not a soil parent spared from a plant's estimate or from the map's plant cells: its frame is between the plant and the sun.
  - A **plant** does not shade the grid cells inside its own footprint, and neither does a `holds_plants` soil solid whose outline contains it: the map's cell at a plant's centre equals the plant's own estimate, on the ground and in a raised bed, so no dark dot sits under a plant (previously 0 h in a cell the estimate called 10 h). Its shadow still shades every cell outside its footprint.
  - These three change existing plans' hours-of-sun maps toward more sun; the release note says so. ADR-059's addendum records the decision and the re-measured shadow-agreement table; `scripts/measure_built_world_shadows.py --check` holds the builders to it.

- **FR-SUN-17**: One region-seam rule for the weighted map (issue #1007)

  - The weighted path paints each transmittance region on its own; a cell whose centre lies exactly on a seam is classified by the path that painted it, so it could take two regions' darker value (one 15-minute sample darker) or neither (a sunny cell inside a shade). Presence is taken from the union — the one path the solid sweep always painted — and a seam cell's value is settled by the even-odd point-in-polygon rule at its centre: the darkest region that contains the centre owns it. A regression test compares a two-region plan's map, sample by sample, with the union's one-path presence and holds a fake painter's double paint and miss to the geometric answer.
